# A 90-second judging walkthrough

Open https://sky-atlas-web.vercel.app/#home and choose **Start a 90-second mission**.

## 0–15 seconds: the problem

“NASA publishes rich infrared observations, but opening an archive is not the
same as understanding what changed. Our atlas turns two real observations into
a small investigation anyone can try.”

## 15–40 seconds: show the evidence

Start Blink. Show the two dates and use Drag to compare. Point out that the
images are actual SPHEREx cutouts on a common sky grid, not estimated animation.
Open the observation passport's IRSA source record if time permits.

## 40–65 seconds: practice scientific judgment

Pin a feature and enter a short observation in the notebook. Add an alternative
explanation such as saturation or different effective wavelengths. Complete the
mission. Explain that the app teaches people to investigate a change without
treating every visual difference as a discovery.

## 65–80 seconds: make it portable

Download the evidence card: it contains both images, dates, observation IDs,
credits and limitations. The notebook exports the visitor's notes with sources.
Open the comet page to show the separate explorer of 167 published predicted
positions, clearly labelled as predictions rather than image detections.

## 80–90 seconds: impact and next step

“This is a bridge from curiosity to evidence. The next research step is matching
wavelengths, masking quality flags and validating alignment before measuring
motion or brightness.”

## Honest boundaries

- There are two real Barnard's Star preview frames, not an all-sky image search.
- The bright target is affected by saturation; effective wavelengths are not
  matched and the two releases differ.
- No precise proper motion, calibrated photometry or new object discovery is
  claimed.
- Comet positions come from the IRSA mission table; they are predicted positions.
- Local storage keeps notebook text in the current browser; shared links contain
  display settings, not notebook contents.

Use **Present** to simplify the layout while showing the viewer. The mission's
final step reveals the notebook again so visitors can record their conclusions.
