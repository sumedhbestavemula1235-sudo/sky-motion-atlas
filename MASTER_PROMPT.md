## Data sources, scientific cautions, and accessibility

The official 2026 Space Apps challenge page is at `https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/`, with the challenges catalog under `/2026/challenges/`. IRSA's 3I/ATLAS page is `https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/SPHEREx/docs/comet3iatlas/index.html`. It links a VOTable listing SPHEREx exposures, comet coordinates at each exposure, and download scripts. IRSA notes low signal-to-noise at many wavelengths and that the comet is spatially extended in some images. The figure itself illustrates infrared coma composition and is **not** a timed motion sequence.

SPHEREx has six detector bands, nominally D1 0.75–1.09 μm, D2 1.10–1.62 μm, D3 1.63–2.41 μm, D4 2.42–3.82 μm, D5 3.83–4.41 μm, and D6 4.42–5.00 μm, with 102 channels total. The current educational band explorer reflects these approximate ranges. A crucial point from official documentation is that SPHEREx has a linear variable filter: wavelength depends on position within a calibrated detector image. Same detector label alone does not guarantee a pixel at the target is at the exact same wavelength in two exposures. Scientific comparison needs matched effective wavelength and sound treatment of WCS, flags, saturation, zodiacal foreground, point-spread function, and intensity scale. Barnard's Star is bright enough that saturation may complicate centroiding, as other Space Apps teams observed in public results. A nearby star moves about 10 arcseconds per year, which at several arcseconds per SPHEREx pixel is subtle. The app must not exaggerate measured displacement without calling it illustrative. The 3I/ATLAS image pairs could be more visually interesting but need real exposure selection, matching sky coverage, wavelength, and a visual test. Comet RA and Dec vary with time, so comparing different visits around fixed celestial coordinates is a more complex problem than comparing a proper-motion star.

The site should remain accessible on desktop and mobile, with legible text, buttons that can be reached by keyboard, labels for sliders, obvious play/pause state, accessible canvas descriptions or accompanying data text, and reduced-motion support. Educational cards should be engaging but not imply that stylized animations are telescope imagery. The animation should not overwhelm the interactive science. DataLink records might return access URLs whose semantics fields or authentication characteristics differ from assumptions.
